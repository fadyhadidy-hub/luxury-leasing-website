                          Model Assumptions & Caveats

                          • US federal tax: 2025 brackets, standard deduction of $15,000 (single) / $30,000 (MFJ). Itemised deductions, ACA PTC, child tax credit, and state-source adjustments are not modelled.
                          • FICA: 6.2% Social Security on wages up to $176,100 and 1.45% Medicare (unlimited). An additional 0.9% Medicare surtax applies to earnings over $200,000 single / $250,000 MFJ.
                          • Texas: No state income tax. Dallas combined sales tax is 8.25% — the model estimates the burden on taxable discretionary spend (groceries are largely exempt in TX).
                          • 401(k): Employee deferral is pre-tax for income tax but NOT for FICA. The 2025 elective deferral limit is $23,500 and the model caps contributions accordingly.
                          • UK income tax: 2025/26 rates and bands for England, Wales & Northern Ireland (Scotland has different bands). Personal allowance tapers £1-for-£2 above £100,000 — creating a 60% effective marginal rate between £100,000 and £125,140.
                          • National Insurance: Class 1 employee, 8% between the primary threshold (£12,570) and UEL (£50,270), then 2% above.
                          • UK workplace pension: Modelled as salary sacrifice — contributions reduce income tax and NI base (most employers run plans this way).
                          • Healthcare: Dallas assumes employer-sponsored plan with employee premium contributions pre-tax via Section 125. London assumes NHS coverage funded through NI/general taxation, with optional private top-up.
                          • VAT: London prices are inclusive of 20% VAT by law. The model surfaces embedded VAT on standard-rated discretionary spend for transparency only — it is already captured in the monthly cost figures.
                          • No tax equalization: Per requirements, the model does NOT apply any employer tax equalization or hypothetical-tax policy. Each city's gross is taxed under that country's domestic regime.
                          • Not modelled: Social Security / NI totalization agreement mechanics, state unemployment tax, FBAR / FATCA compliance costs, US worldwide taxation on foreign earned income (FEIE / foreign tax credit), stamp duty on home purchase, inheritance tax, capital gains, ISA vs Roth optimisation, health FSA, dependent care FSA, and cross-border pension portability.
                          • All figures are illustrative. Verify every number with a cross-border tax advisor before making a relocation decision.
